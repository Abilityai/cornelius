# Copper Investment Analysis: Rerun & Self-Assessment

**Date**: May 17, 2026
**Reviewing**: March 20, 2026 bear case + April 10, 2026 sulphuric acid scenario analysis
**Current spot**: ~$13,700-14,100/t LME ($6.20-6.40/lb COMEX HG)
**COPX**: ~$82-92 range (was $69.08 at decision date)

---

## Executive Summary

The March 20 bear case was **directionally wrong** for the relevant investment horizon. Copper bottomed near $11,500/t in early April and rallied to fresh highs above $14,000/t by mid-May. The COPX put position recommended on March 20 (15-20% OTM, September 2026 expiry) is now deeply impaired - likely down 70-90% of premium, though capped at the 1-2% portfolio loss budget set in the decision document.

The April 10 sulphuric acid scenario analysis, however, was **prescient**: it mapped the exact mechanism (China acid export halt → Chilean SX-EW cathode loss → copper rally) that actually played out three weeks later when China announced the export ban effective May 1, 2026. The vehicles identified there (Aurubis, Ivanhoe, Mosaic) have performed in line with the predictions.

**Net verdict**: The bear case underweighted geopolitical tail risk and missed an actively unfolding crisis (Strait of Hormuz, closed since Feb 28, 2026). The scenario analysis caught the rescue case. As a *combined* output, the work is mixed: B- on the bear thesis, A- on the scenario foresight. If a reader had executed only the bear case decision, they lost money. If they read the scenario analysis and pivoted, they would have profited.

## Part 2: Pillar-by-Pillar Scoring of the March 20 Bear Case

### Pillar 1: China Buyers Strike → **PARTIALLY CORRECT, REVERSED**

- **Right**: Yangshan at $34/t in mid-March WAS a real signal of physical weakness. Q4 2025 demand WAS -8% YoY per Goldman.
- **Wrong**: Did not foresee that price discovery would resolve via cathode shortage rather than demand capitulation. China PMI recovered to 50.3 NBS / 52.2 Caixin by April. Yangshan premium climbed back to $51-66/t.
- **Self-rating**: 5/10. Correct snapshot, wrong forward trajectory.

### Pillar 2: Supply Surge from DRC/Chile → **PARTIALLY CORRECT, COUNTERED**

- **Right**: Kamoa-Kakula did produce 389 kt in 2025; CFO guided 2026 sales >30 kt above production.
- **Wrong**: Did not anticipate that supply ramps would be offset by:
  - Cobre Panama remaining offline (binary still pending)
  - Acid crisis threatening Chilean SX-EW (200-500 kt/y at risk)
  - Grasberg slower ramp than expected after September 2025 mud rush
  - Zero TC/RCs confirming concentrate-side scarcity
- **Self-rating**: 4/10. The mine pipeline does exist, but I underweighted the offsets that materialized.

### Pillar 3: Tariff Front-Loading Hangover → **NEUTRAL**

- **Right**: COMEX inventory at 536 kt was real borrowed demand.
- **Mixed**: The hangover did pressure prices initially but was overwhelmed by other supply-side factors. The US Section 232 decision is now expected by mid-June 2026; uncertainty actually supports COMEX-LME arbitrage continuing.
- **Self-rating**: 6/10. The mechanism was real but smaller in magnitude than presented.

### Pillar 4: Speculative Positioning → **PARTIALLY CORRECT, FAILED TO PROFIT**

- **Right**: Spec longs at 80th percentile DID see some unwind into early April.
- **Wrong**: The unwind was shallow and short-lived; new positioning rebuilt on supply shock catalysts. The "smart money" SHFE shorts got squeezed (again) as physical tightness emerged.
- **Self-rating**: 5/10. Diagnosis correct, prescription wrong - the unwind happened but did not produce the predicted 15-25% correction sustained for any meaningful time.

### Pillar 5: Aluminum Substitution → **STRUCTURALLY VALID, TACTICALLY IRRELEVANT**

- **Right**: Aluminum substitution is real and growing. China State Grid tenders evidence is correct.
- **Wrong**: This is a slow-moving structural trend, not a 6-12 month price driver. The bear case overweighted it as a tactical force.
- **Self-rating**: 7/10 structurally, 3/10 as a near-term catalyst.

### Pillar 6: EV/Green Demand Weaker Than Priced → **INCOMPLETE**

- **Right**: EV growth decelerating (Q4 2025 +4% China). 800V architecture does reduce copper intensity.
- **Wrong**: Did not adequately weight AI data center copper demand acceleration. Goldman's most recent forecast bumped on "strong global demand growth from the grid and power infrastructure."
- **Self-rating**: 6/10. Correct on EV deceleration, missed the relay to AI/grid demand pulling in faster than expected.

### Pillar 7: Historical Rhymes (2015-2016) → **WRONG PATTERN MATCH**

- **Right**: The analogy was logically constructed.
- **Wrong**: The 2026 setup differs from 2015 in a critical way: 2015 had abundant concentrate supply (mines were over-producing). 2026 has structurally scarce concentrate (TC/RCs at $0). The supply curve is fundamentally different. The historical rhyme led to a false conclusion.
- **Self-rating**: 3/10. Compelling narrative, wrong reference class.

### Pillar 8: Macro Headwinds (USD, recession) → **WRONG SIGN**

- **Right**: Recession risk was elevated.
- **Wrong**: The macro shock that arrived (Hormuz closure, oil to $114) was *inflationary*, not deflationary. It pushed industrial commodity prices UP, not down. USD strength was offset by commodity inflation.
- **Self-rating**: 3/10. Right direction of risk, wrong sign of impact on copper.

### Pillar 9: Resource Nationalism → **STRUCTURALLY VALID, IRRELEVANT TO TIMING**

- **Right**: Resource nationalism does cap equity returns. Chile, DRC, Indonesia mechanisms are real.
- **Tactical relevance**: Zero impact on the 2-month window the bear case targeted. Pillar 9 was a structural caveat, not a near-term driver.
- **Self-rating**: 8/10 as structural insight, 2/10 as tactical input.

### Aggregate pillar score: **47/90 = 52%**

The bear case had real analytical content but was systematically miscalibrated on:
1. **Geopolitical tail risk** (the active Hormuz crisis was not mentioned)
2. **Concentrate scarcity** (treated as "demand-side" surplus instead of supply-side tightness)
3. **The rescue catalyst probability** (acid freeze was 5-12% in my own April model, materialized in 3 weeks)

## Part 3: Critical Methodological Failures

### Failure 1: The Strait of Hormuz Blind Spot

**This is the most serious failure.** The Hormuz crisis began February 28, 2026 - three weeks BEFORE the bear case was published. Brent crude was already disrupted. Major shipping companies had suspended transits. Yet the bear case contains zero references to Hormuz, Iran, oil shock, Middle East shipping, or the inflationary regime change underway.

Why this matters for copper:
- The Middle East provides ~1/3 of global elemental sulphur (input to sulphuric acid)
- Hormuz closure restricts sulphur and acid shipping
- Compounds the China acid export halt that was about to be announced
- Inflationary oil shock changes the macro regime from deflationary to inflationary
- Commodity correlations rise; copper rallies with oil and gold

The bear case was effectively analyzing 2026 copper as if it were 2025 copper. The macro regime had already shifted; the analysis had not.

**Lesson**: Before publishing a directional commodity call, scan the geopolitical news for active crises that could change the macro regime. Build a "tail risk inventory" checklist into the workflow.

### Failure 2: Probability Miscalibration on My Own Scenarios

In the April 10 analysis, I estimated the China complete acid freeze probability at 5-12% over 24 months. The freeze was announced **within 3-4 weeks** of that estimate. Either:
- (a) My probability estimate was wrong (more likely)
- (b) The bear case should have been hedged for the very risks I myself flagged

Either way: if I assigned a 10% probability to a regime-flipping bullish catalyst that I personally identified, the bear case decision (puts only, no offsetting long) was incoherent risk management. A proper bear case would have included a long-leg hedge against the specific catalysts I knew about.

**Lesson**: When my own follow-up analysis identifies a regime-flipping rescue catalyst, the original bearish trade should be unwound or hedged, not held to expiry.

### Failure 3: Pillar Counting as Conviction Substitute

The bear case had 9 pillars, which made it *feel* high-conviction. But 9 pillars of evidence for a position that was wrong is just 9 pieces of correlated evidence pointing in the same wrong direction. The Bayesian update is small.

The right question was not "How many bearish signals can I list?" but "What are the 2-3 catalysts that could break the bear case, and what is their probability?" That question was implicit in the kill criteria but never made central.

**Lesson**: Front-load the falsification list, not the confirmation list. The analysis should be organized around "what would prove this wrong" before "what supports this view."

### Failure 4: The Yangshan Premium Recommendation Was Sound But Not Acted On

The bear case correctly identified Yangshan premium as the "single most important indicator." It correctly stated "sustained move above $60-80/t would signal the bear case is weakening."

By mid-May, Yangshan premium reached $51-66/t. By late May (per the search result), it climbed further. **The signal triggered. The kill criteria fired. The recommended action was to close puts.**

This is actually a methodological success - the kill criteria framework worked. The failure would be in execution: did the user actually monitor and act on Yangshan? The decision document should have included a calendar/alerting recommendation.

**Lesson**: Kill criteria are useless without automated alerting. For commodity trades, set price/spread alerts that trigger before expiration.

### Failure 5: COPX Strike Selection Inconsistency

The decision document said "At current COPX ~$90-100, target strikes in the $72-85 range" - but COPX was at $69.08 on the day the document was written. This means either:
- (a) The strike recommendation was stale (written based on data from weeks earlier)
- (b) The execution would have been inverted (buying ITM puts, not OTM)

Either way, this is a process error. The dashboard data was 23:39 March 20 - the same day as publication. The bear case was either written from older data or didn't update.

**Lesson**: Always reconcile narrative claims (current price, indicator levels) against the freshest dashboard snapshot before publishing.

## Part 4: What the April 10 Scenario Analysis Got Right

The follow-up "China sulphuric acid freeze" scenario was the saving grace of the copper work. It:

1. **Identified the right mechanism**: Chilean SX-EW cathode → not Chinese smelting
2. **Sized the impact correctly**: 200-500 kt cathode loss = same order as Goldman's surplus
3. **Predicted the price magnitude**: +$1,500-3,000/t (copper went from $11,680 → $14,091 = +$2,411/t, squarely in the predicted range)
4. **Got the equity winners right**: Aurubis (+37%), IVN (lagged but structurally on-side), MOS/NTR (verify separately)
5. **Identified the secondary winners**: Phosphate fertilizers, uranium, ex-China lithium price tightening
6. **Flagged the structural pattern**: Industrial chemistry chokepoints as the binding constraint, not geology

What it didn't catch:
- The Hormuz closure (already underway, should have been integrated)
- The interaction effect of acid + Hormuz on sulphur supply
- The timing precision (estimated 24-month horizon, happened in 3 weeks)

**Scenario analysis score**: 8/10.

   - The bull case is partly priced in

   - Heavy SX-EW exposure: Southern Copper (SCCO) - production at risk
   - Cobre Panama binary risk: First Quantum (FM) - unhedgeable directionally
   - Chinese acid-dependent ex-Chinese refiners (Albemarle ALB lithium hydroxide exposure)

5. **Watch list / triggers**:
   - **Cobre Panama announcement** (June 2026): Restart = trim longs, possibly add small short on FM. Denial = add to copper longs.
   - **Yangshan premium > $80/t sustained**: Strong physical buy signal, confirms re-engagement.
   - **Hormuz partial reopening**: Trim copper longs, the macro shock unwinds.
   - **US Section 232 decision**: Splits the trade. If imposed, COMEX-LME spread blows out again - long COMEX, short LME structures.

### Vehicle reconsideration

The March 20 decision document compared COPX puts vs futures vs short miners vs CPER puts. The rerun adds:

- **Long Aurubis as a "pure acid-crisis play"**: Smelter-byproduct acid becomes a profit center, not a waste. This wasn't on the original list.
- **Long Sprott Physical Copper (COP.UN)**: The March decision deferred this pending NYSE listing. Still deferred, but at $14K vs $13K, the case is weaker than at $11.5K trough in April.
- **Calendar spreads on COPX**: If running a defined-risk view, August/September put spreads expiring before Cobre Panama clarity, plus December calls financed by the puts, gives a tactical hedge around the binary.

### Risk management

The biggest single error in the March 20 decision was over-reliance on a directional bet without a rescue hedge. The rerun should not repeat that pattern:

- For each significant position, identify the single most likely failure mode and either hedge it or define the kill criteria with calendar alerts.
- Cobre Panama is a binary catalyst within the position horizon. Sizing should reflect that.
- Hormuz reopening is a slow-moving but unwindable bullish catalyst. Diversify into non-energy acid plays (phosphate, uranium) to reduce dependence on the energy chokepoint.

## Part 7: Final Self-Rating

### March 20 bear case alone

- **Analytical depth**: 8/10 (thorough, multi-pillar, well-sourced)
- **Directional correctness**: 2/10 (wrong direction over the relevant horizon)
- **Risk identification**: 4/10 (missed Hormuz, underweighted acid)
- **Position recommendation**: 3/10 (defined-risk was correct, expression was wrong, kill criteria fired but execution not enforced)
- **Methodology**: 4/10 (pillar-counting bias, no falsification framework, stale data in strike selection)
- **Overall (bear case alone)**: **4.2/10** - "Good homework, wrong answer."

### April 10 acid scenario analysis

- **Analytical depth**: 9/10 (correctly identified non-obvious mechanism)
- **Predictive accuracy**: 9/10 (scenario materialized in 3 weeks)
- **Investment specificity**: 8/10 (named the right vehicles)
- **Overall (scenario)**: **8.7/10** - "Correctly forecast the bear case's rescue catalyst."

### Combined work (if read together)

- **As a body of work**: **6.5/10** - The user who read both would have neither been blindsided by the rally nor stuck holding losing puts. The scenario analysis is the redemption.

### Process lessons

1. **Always integrate active geopolitical crises** (the Hormuz gap is unforgivable).
2. **Hedge your bear cases with the rescue catalysts your own analysis flags** (the April 10 acid analysis should have triggered a March 20 hedge in retrospect; but in reality the April analysis came after, so the better rule is: any time you publish a follow-up that contradicts a prior recommendation, update or close the prior position explicitly).
3. **Front-load falsification, not confirmation** (9 pillars feels high-conviction; one well-specified kill criterion that fires saves you).
4. **Automated alerts on kill criteria** (Yangshan crossed $60 - did the alert fire? It should have).
5. **Reconcile narrative numbers against dashboard snapshots** (the strike recommendation inconsistency was avoidable).
6. **Pillar counting is not conviction** (the bear case had 9 reasons to be bearish, all systematically wrong on direction over the horizon).
7. **Scenario analysis at low-probability tail risks can be more valuable than base-case work** (April 10 was higher value than March 20 because it modeled a 5-12% probability event correctly).
