# Value Trap vs Clean Torque: Why the Highest-Cost Name Isn't Automatically the Best Bet

The operating-leverage thesis ([[operating-leverage-cost-curve-torque]]) says the worst-margin producer has the most torque to the commodity price. True - but high-cost producers are frequently high-cost *because something is wrong with the asset or the jurisdiction*, and that same something can decouple the stock from the commodity entirely. The torque only pays out if the idiosyncratic risk is absent or resolves.

Three distinct failure modes hide inside "high cost":

1. **Value trap (binary/operational):** the cost is high because the asset is broken or a binary event hangs over it. First Quantum was the worst-margin copper name *and* the highest-binary-risk name (Cobre Panama shut, ~40% of capacity offline). Through the overhang it traded as a bet on Panama politics, not on copper. When the overhang began to clear (May 2026 audit, restart imminent) its latent leverage delivered the single highest return in the universe (+46% in a month). Maximum torque is maximum reward when the overhang lifts and maximum pain when it doesn't.

2. **Torque trap (value extraction):** the operating leverage is real but it accrues to someone other than the shareholder. KGHM is genuinely high-cost, but the Polish state copper tax escalates with the copper price and captures the upside - so it underperformed names with identical cost but no tax wedge. Resource-nationalism royalties (Chile, DRC, Zambia, Indonesia) are softer versions. **A high-cost producer in a high-extraction jurisdiction is a trap: the torque is harvested by the government.**

3. **The by-product illusion (the inverse trap):** a name can screen as *low*-cost yet still be high-torque - because its low net cash cost is built from gold/silver/moly credits, not efficiency, and its torque comes from those by-products plus financial leverage. Hudbay (negative net cash cost, highest beta, +42%) is copper-adjacent torque from a different engine - and it decays as the by-product grade depletes.

The filter that separates **clean torque** from these traps: high gross cash cost + high commodity-revenue share (~90%+, so the torque is genuinely the target commodity) + no disqualifying binary + low value-extraction wedge. In copper June 2026 that pointed to Capstone, Ero, and Teck as clean torque; First Quantum as a resolving binary kicker; KGHM and Ivanhoe as traps to avoid as proxies.

The deeper rule: **operating leverage is necessary but not sufficient. Always ask where the torque goes and whether anything stands between the cost curve and the share price.**
