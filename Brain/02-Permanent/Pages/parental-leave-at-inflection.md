# Parental leave at Inflection   
## Context   
When I first started at Inflection, our first child, Fitz, was less than a year old, and we had agreed as part of the contract that I could take three months full-time, full pay, parenting with him. Since introducing Fitz to daycare ended up going very smoothly, I was just "off" for one month before returning to work. However, having time alone with my son, and making sure Julia's career got re-started was very important to me, even if we didn't need the full 3 months.   
   
As you're all probably aware by now, we're expecting a second child at the end of this year. The ETA is 12th of December, but as we know there's some uncertainty baked into this estimate. As part of this, we thought it makes sense to establish some frame around how to deal with paternal leave (maternal leave might need other frames, biologically speaking) at inflection, given the size of the firm, and the individual responsibility of us to manage our own work.   
   
We want to enable our team members to experience early parenthood in a way that fits their situation. This might look different legally, culturally, and based on the role, hence I don't think we should prescribe the exact implementation of parental leave. But, we are setting some form of precedent for employees to come here.    
   
## Guiding principles   
Actually the principles of how we decide on how to implement parental leave aren't that different from our core values as a firm: authenticity, care, craft, creativity, freedom and fun. Just that they are applied to parenting as well. In addition and in line with our values, I'd like to add two guiding principles for this specific situation:   
1. **Flexibility**: adapting both to the needs of the family (first) and the company (second) during parental leave. Both in total time commitment, focus and responsiveness. We expect flexibility both on the individual side, as well as our colleagues'.   
2. **Responsibility**: (de-)prioritizing ongoing company responsibilities such as portfolio work or investments during the time away, or delegating critical things; not leaving anyone or anything hanging. Taking on new responsibility as a parent requires dedicated time.   
3. **Health:** taking care of ourselves and loved ones as required is required to do any good work. For men, this is less of a concern, but for both child and mother, stress during pregnancy can be very dangerous. For men, most of the stress is second hand or once the baby is born (poor sleep etc.) in my experience.   
   
   
## How to apply this for JLB: Proposal   
**1 month post birth: Full parental leave**   
Starting when the baby is born (can theoretically be anytime from now until the 22nd of December) until 4 weeks forward, I would like to be fully focused on getting to know the new baby, and taking care of Julia and Fitz. Ideally, that will be over Christmas + first two weeks of January, where things are very quiet anyway.    
**2 months post birth: flexible leave/prioritized work**   
After having been fully off for a month, I will start to get back into some form of work routine again, checking emails, taking occasional calls, et c. How much this will be will depend very much on how everyone is doing, and what the urgency is on the inflection-side. Focus should be on doing the most important work, like fundraising, investment decisions, and portfolio fundraises in my case. While making sure everyone is good at home of course.   
**2-13 months post birth: normal work**   
After the adjustment period, things should have settled a bit. Julia will be off work for at least 12-14 months, taking care of the baby and I can get back to work, though I will have to limit time away from home even more.   
**13-16 months out post birth: flexible leave**   
*Potentially*, once Julia wants to start work again, I would take over childcare until he starts daycare. We haven't decided on how/when/if exactly this will happen. I really enjoyed the time home alone with Fitz as a small baby when Julia started work, and helping him get settled in daycare. Should we choose to do this again, then I'd like to take the time required for that. I can't say exactly how much or when it will be, but just that it may come. *I suggest we take that discussion when it happens.*   
   
### Financial considerations   
Here's what my original employment contract said:   
> (3) The employee shall be entitled to parental leave for three (3) months with continued payment of the remuneration pursuant to § 4 subsection 1. Parental leave is granted at the earliest at the end of the probationary period and must be completed by December 31st, 2022. Parental allowance shall be offset against the remuneration. The Federal Parental Allowance and Parental Leave Act shall apply to parental leave requests exceeding this.   

    
Option 1:   
- Full pay minus any state contributions for a total of three months/child as described in the clause above → This would then include months 1, 2 and potentially months 13/14 for me   
   
   
Option 2:   
- Full pay minus any state contributions for a total of three months/employee → I'd have two more months to "use" up, would be months 1, and 2. For month 13/14 I'd forego any salary   
   
   
Option 3:   
- This clause was only for 2022 and now it's 2025, we can decide on a new "standard". e.g., anywhere between 0 and 100% salary for x months   
   
   
I didn't end up taking my three months before the end of 2022 because we didn't need it, but I suggest we extend the clause to every current and future team member. It's generous, but limited. For inflection the main constraint is time and availability, not necessarily a months salary more or less. We don't care when the work happens, or how many hours was spent doing it; it's in our culture that we do what is needed to get the work done. In that sense it feels wrong to me to say we reduce pay drastically because a team member happen to not be working one month. This should not be abused obviously, which is why we limit it to 3 months.
