# Future of computing   
I do believe the chip industry is definitely going towards more specialization, short term, as we're coming up against the techno-economic limits of Moore's law. The question is how the specialization will happen and what technologies will provide the path of least resistance for an industry with huge amounts of strategic momentum in a certain direction. Short term (3-5y), I believe we'll see two types of (HW) performance improvements:   
1. General purpose chips running on higher processes (e.g., Apple getting dedicated access to TSMC 2N) in combination with higher focus on GPU-CPU integrations for AI workloads   
2. Specialized architectures on lesser (but still <12N) process nodes that become cheaper as some incremental innovation is added and chip design becomes more accessible. Drivers may be edge AI, and a growing wearables market   
   
Demand for both consumer and datacenter business will continue to be massive and have double digit CAGR. Technologies involved here that I expect will become (more) prevalent are things like chiplets, advanced packaging (especially 3D), slight deviations from von Neumann architectures and perhaps some analog computing. RISC-V ISA is something that seems to be prevailing and so we may place an investment on that.   
Longer term (5-15y), I believe traditional silicon will be overtaken by one or more of disruptive paradigms, such as photonic, quantum (to a small extent on an absolute scale, and only for very specific problems), quantum-inspired classical computing, neuromorphic at scale. I don't think these long term, step function advances will come by just "cranking up the power" and stacking more and more chips on smaller and smaller areas. We will reach fundamental limits of the materials we use, in heat dissipation, resistance, etc. Probably we'll need to rethink the transistor from first principles.   
Even further down the line we might see things like generalized quantum computing, adiabatic computing approaching the Landauer limit within 500x at useful speeds. But this is still pretty far out in terms of practicability and market momentum.   
Only a few of these longer to very long term technologies have to work out for computing to change fundamentally. They will require massive innovation and investment in new materials, manufacturing and process technology, lithography, digital design tools, and infrastructure to support these new compute speeds (from languages to interconnects).
