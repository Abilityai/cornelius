# European Defense Tech Hub [name tbd]   
# Motivation   
Europe is becoming more fragile because of new macro-scale parameters, more entrenched dependencies, and a distraction of our brightest minds. We are facing a repeat emergence of great military forces and territorial greed from Russia, while the U.S. turns inward and is likely to elect an outspoken "America-first", unpredictable criminal for president. China, Iran and their allies are strengthening collaborations to gain strategic advantages over the U.S. and Europe through disinformation and proxy wars. Europe has some of the best universities in the world, produce software unicorns and groundbreaking research, but we are still highly dependent on American capital, Taiwanese semiconductors and African rare earth minerals. The energy supply to run the  factories we have is dependent on Russian gas, or Chinese solar cells. For a long time, startups have been actively discouraged from selling to government because of insanely long sales cycles and difficult access, leaving the most important democratic institutions with technology from incumbent, slow-moving corporates. This has led to an oversupply of "B2B SaaS" companies because they have been seen as a proven business model with low CapEx. Venture investors turned away from tech and market risk and towards social networks.   
In Ukraine, the rate of innovation was determined by necessity. The only way to beat a much stronger opponent is by being more agile and do things the other side can't. As our partners at the Ukrainian MoD said "We should have lost in the first week, by all logic.". Then came the drone revolution, and citizen engagement. It was an advantage which was quickly countered, with jamming, GPS-denial, counter-drone offensives and EW-targeting artillery. But this only triggered further innovation, in terminal guidance drones, improved networking and communications protocol, widespread employment of Starlink, and rapid scaleup of drones. This cat-and-mouse game works in favor of the innovating entity, the side that can maintain momentum, but it's very different from the long-term needs of the European forces.   
Even though there is improvement on the political engagement with defense and investment therein, both on an EU-level and a national one with increased budgets for the EDF, and Sweden joining NATO, we believe those initiatives are moving too slowly and fail to engage startups efficiently.    
Hence, we propose to launch the **European Defense Tech Hub** in Munich.   
# A hub for European defense tech startups   
Building startups with the military or government as a customer requires different skills and support than traditional entrepreneurial efforts. Very few founders know the user, even fewer know the purchaser, and engaging with potential users and getting their feedback is prohibitively difficult. Once there is a product idea, testing and developing that product may be subject to severe security restrictions, and once the product is ready, selling it may be subject to export restrictions. The same applies outside of the military, to areas traditionally expected to be supplied by government related to the functioning of society, such as infrastructure security, access to compute, and internet resilience.    
> We want to enable the best entrepreneurs to work on the most important problems for the security of Europe, and make sure they have the conditions to become the next generation of defense primes.   

   
## Founders   
We explicitly target founders that will give Europe a strategic advantage in future conflicts, with the use of advanced technologies. The selection of participants will be extremely careful, with a focus on quality over quantity. We are targeting founding personas who are not conforming to traditional norms. They should be extremely ambitious and resilient, with very high tolerance for risk, and scary intelligent. We subscribe to [MEI](https://scale.com/blog/meritocracy-at-scale). There are plenty of accelerator programs for those who want to build traditional companies.    
Qualifying criteria to apply are that each team have:   
- At least 2 co-founders, but not more than 3   
- An initial product direction and short write-up   
- Not yet raised more than EUR2 M venture funding   
- Willingness and ability to spend 3 months in Munich   
- Willingness, if suitable, to test their product in Ukraine   
   
   
We would suggest to take fewer companies in, with high quality, as the first cohort will set the standard for others applying. The goal is to take in ca 3-7 companies in the first cohort, and only take the very best. Ideally we have over 1000 applicants to select from.   
   
### Focus areas   
Companies that build products that make European democracy stronger are encouraged, be that in active conflict or in peace times. We don't want to limit the program to one specific set of technologies, such as drones, but rather include the science fiction ideas for how to:   
- deter invasion through superiority   
- ensure the information environment stays secure   
- protect critical infrastructure from all kinds of threats (cyber, kinetic, environmental)   
   
   
## Format and activities   
The hub is for founders, and the format and activities are set up to give them the maximal benefit. We structure this around three types of activities:   
1. Community building through physical **events**   
2. Entrepreneurship support and company building through **acceleration and investment**   
3. Creating awareness of opportunities in defense tech through **publications**   
   
   
### Events   
We will continue to organize hackathons and conferences for entrepreneurs, investors, public sector and academia to exchange. Hackathons bring together the exploratory element of early product-market fit search with targeted marketing, recruitment and technical research for industry or public sector partners.    
**→ We've successfully organized the largest defense tech hackathon in the world, in Munich **   
Conferences will serve the participants interested in strategic discussions raher than tactical building or immediate problem solving, and networking.   
**→ We organized a Defense conference with over 150 participants at the Festival of the Future, in Munich**   
After both events, we received lare amounts of positive feedback and requests for future events as well as more support. We attracted top engineering, scientific, entrepreneurial and military talent to engage significant resources in our events. The event was entirely funded through sponsorship from entities like Auterion, Helsing, Quantum-Systems, Founders@uniBW.   
   
### Acceleration and investment   
The main factor of success for founders to find product-market fit is to have short feedback loops between product iterations and user feedback and understanding. This is impossible for 99% of defense tech founders as they can not easily collect insights from government clients. We can help them bridge that gap.   
The acceleration will be a **3-month program in Munich** for founding **teams** who have a product idea, perhaps a prototype, but no venture funding, nor is already actively selling to a defense client.   
**The content **will be set up to train founders in all the basics of building a startup, including how to identify product-market fit, how to set up a company, how to scale a team, how to fundraise, etc. But most importantly, it will be focused on building prototypes and gathering feedback from military users and purchasers in order to ensure that they build the right product.    
The 3 months end with a demo day, on an invite-only basis to expose the companies to investors and potential clients. We will partner with the Ukrainian MoD to enable testing in the field in Ukraine.   
During the program, the founders will receive a stipend to cover some living costs. After the program, there will be an option for **investment from Inflection**. The investment will be one check of $125k for 8% and a second tranche of $125k with MFN.   
Inflection has been investing into sovereign compute companies since 2019 through two funds, with areas covering novel compute architectures, cryptography, aerospace and robotics   
To make a defense company truly venture investable, we believe it will need to either have very large coverage within Europe, sell to the US, or have a great commercial go-to-market. The defense market only-approach is relatively small in Europe at this point.   
   
### Publications   
The public is not well educated on the importance of defense tech for the sovereignty of Europe. To counter this we will continue to publish articles, research pieces and interviews with founders, general staff across Europe and politicians.   
We have been running newsletters and blogs with thousands of subscribers: SVRGN and the Future of Computing blog   
The goal of publishing media for EDTH is to encourage new talent to flow into the field, and give ideas and insights into defense tech to founders. We also want to showcase the startup successes coming out of the acceleration program, but not exclusively those. We have unique access to startups and investors in this space, and can get interviews and depth that traditional media can not reach.   
   
## The partners and operators   
As founding partners we want aligned parties whose interest it is to help build the next European defense primes. We can not have corporates who would compete with startups in the acceleration program. They may be involved as media-partners or in sharing insights on a case-by-case basis.    
The inital set would be Inflection, TUM venture labs and Benjamin Wolba + Cyrus Hesabi   
Here are some initial suggestions for responsibilities we could take over:   
- Inflection   
    - startup selection   
    - entrepreneurial coaching   
    - **investment decisions and running investment vehicle**   
    - partner involvement and outreach towards other European hubs   
- TUM Venture labs   
    - talent sourcing from academia   
    - scientific input and collaboration   
    - entrepreneurial coaching   
    - engagement with local partners (e.g., Bavarian state ministry)   
- Benjamin+Cyrus   
    - Media production and publication   
    - Hackathon coordination   
    - Community engagement   
- Ukraine MoD   
    - Acceleration support   
    - Mentors from general staff and UA startup ecosystem   
    - Access to testing grounds    
   
   
We are also exploring involving EF further, as they have experience in running accelerators and could have operational resources as well as investment resources to dedicate towards EDTH. Other partners who could be interesting to explore partnership with are:   
- Venture funds: not great because of misaligned incentives   
- Public institutions: not great because or bureaucracy   
- NATO: actually great if we get freedom to operate   
- Scale-ups e.g., anduril, helsing: likely to acquire or steal IP   
   
Some of these are great to involve but keep at an arms-length distance and warn  founders about dangers of working with these partners.   
   
## Legal and capital requirements   
It would make sense to set up a joint venture or a fresh entity for the EDTH to manage resources and employ operational people. [open to suggestions on how this would look exactly].   
Inflection would set up a dedicated investment vehicle and raise money for that, likely ~$5M initially as a micro fund. We could consider assigning a degree of carry of that vehicle to the operational entity or the individuals most heavily contributing to the success of the hub.    
We'll need to cover quite significant costs for running the program and operations. Namely: floor space, employment costs, and activities around it. There are the following options to pay for such expenses:   
- Public funding through the Bavarian state ministry    
    - + value aligned capital and neutral   
    - + non-profit status helps   
    - - might come with strings attached   
    - - might reduce "European"-focus   
- Set up a vehicle for this and raise venture funding   
    - + value aligned and would cover expenses   
    - - not expected to generate that type of returns   
    - - not neutral, would expect first look at investments   
    - - unclear involvment of the founding parties then   
- Philanthropic funding through family offices/UNHWI   
    - - hard to raise enough capital most likely   
    - - might have strings attached
