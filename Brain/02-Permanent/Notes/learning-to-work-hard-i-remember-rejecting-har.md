# **Learning to work hard**   
I remember rejecting hard intellectual work in my teens and early adult life, thinking I’m too clever to degrade myself to have to work hard. And I could coast on arrogance and mild talent with maths, for some time. But it set me up for a rude awakening and the wrong mentality, once others my age started achieving and I only got by. I wanted the results without trying. Unfortunately, my smarts weren’t smart enough for my ambition to be satisfied. Maybe if I’d do more obviously, famously even, difficult things, like physics, I could prove myself to someone, without having to work so hard. Turns out that wasn’t true either. Not to say I didn’t study or do anything, but what I thought was hard work back then, ie. 40 hours a week of something, wasn’t really. It wasn’t hard work when compared to the velocity and turn around times required in consulting, or when you know there’s no one else who will make it work, who’ll save you and make you successful by default, unless you do it yourself.
